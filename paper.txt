Abstract:
 Deep learning has advanced at a staggering pace, facilitating the production of 24 
near-original deepfakes—video and audio files that impersonate real people convincingly. 25 
These advancements can be beneficial in entertainment, accessibility, and content creation, 26 
however, many of the technological misuse can bring harm relating to misinformation, 27 
digital trickery, Impersonation in cybercrimes, fraud and identity theft. This paper 28 
describes a dual -modality deepfake detection system that targets both video and audio 29 
forgeries through custom deep learning techniques. For the video detection, we suggest a 30 
hybrid CNN-LSTM model which retrieves spatial information of cropped faces and also 31 
tracks temporal changes across sequences. For audio deepfake detection, a lightweight 32 
LSTM architecture with Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) is suggested. Both 33 
models are embedded within a web -based detection platform to  enable practical 34 
application in the real world, allowing users to independently upload and examine media 35 
content for credible authenticity. 36 
For video deepfake detection, the results are evaluated using a composite dataset 37 
comprising FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF (v2), and DFDC facial frame samples, and attained 38 
an accuracy of 94% whereas for audio detection, the SceneFake dataset with different 39 
noise and enhancement levels is used with detection accuracy of 98%. The suggested 40 
methodology works in real-time and can be deployed in non-controlled scenarios without 41 
the need for  specialized hardware. The experimental results validate the robustness of 42

1. Introduction 49 
In regard to the advancement of deep learning and generative models, the existence of 50 
deepfake technologies pose a new more sophisticated challenge towards the verification 51 
and authentication systems of digital media. Deepfakes are self -sufficient clips that 52 
integrate video and audio bound together through Artificial Intelligent systems featuring 53 
exact resemblance to the target individual’s traits and mannerisms. Despite the fact that 54 
the technology could enhance the fields of industry such as entertainment, education, and 55 
even disability aid, the possibility of misinformation, social and content abuse and identity 56 
theft poses serious ethical issues. 57 
One consequence from the misuse of AI is the ability to impersonate to a social identity by 58 
generating falsified content which is going beyond boundaries. The sophisticated nature 59 
of these technologies has propelled deepfake technologies to gain popularity among 60 
amateurs using them for unscrupulous reasons. With access to open -source tools for the 61 
AI deep learning framework, it has become very easy even for novices to create convincing 62 
fake media. As the sophistication of deepfake technologies increase, so does the dosage of 63 
contention which lowers the public trust towards digital content. Basic detection processes 64 
are becoming obsolete, which further highlights the necessity of protective automated 65 
systems that have the ability to detect deepfakes efficiently and within a short amount of 66 
time[1-7]. 67 
Concerning this issue, many researchers have adopted deep learning methods, especially 68 
those employing deep neural networks. In this category, Convolutional Neural Networks 69 
(CNNs) are frequently utilized to spatially extract features from image videos or audio 70 
spectrograms while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) - specifically Long Short -Term 71 
Memory, or LSTM, units - are used to retain temporal and sequential order dependencies. 72 
With the growing threat of deepfakes, detecting manipulated audio and video content has 73 
become increasingly important. Deepfake detection techniques analyze inconsistencies in 74 
speech and visual patterns to identify synthetic media. Features like Mel -Frequency 75 
Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) are extracted to capture key characteristics of speech. These 76 
features are then analyzed using a Long Short -Term Memory (LSTM) model to detect 77 
temporal anomalies typical in fake audio. This work integrates both audio (LSTM-MFCC) 78 
and video (LSTM -CNN) based detection into a unified deepfake identification system. 79 
These types of structures are particularly good at recognizing sophisticated alterations that 80 
escape the notice of even the most attentive humans [8-9]. 81 
In this paper we have suggested a dual -modality deepfake detection framework capable 82 
of handling both audio and video deepfake detection. For video deepfake detection, we 83 
apply a deep learning methodology using CNN -LSTM, where CNNs spatially pool face 84 
Sensors 2025, 25, x FOR PEER REVIEW 3 of 22  
 
frame sequences and LSTMs capture the temporal relationship across video frames. We 85 
have used Faceforensics++ and Celeb-DF datasets for the video detection system. 86 
Audio deepfake detection methods tend to rely on spectral features, prosodic features, or 87 
phase discrepancies to identify real and synthetic speech.  CNNs, autoencoders, and 88 
transformer-based classifiers are  some of the models that are typically 89 
employed to examine these features for detecting forgery. For audio detection, we capture 90 
speech using Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) and then feed them into an 91 
LSTM-based model designed to recognize temporal structures within speech and identify 92 
inconsistencies resulting from synthesis. The model is trained using the SceneFake dataset 93 
which contains audio samples with different signal -to-noise ratios and enhancement 94 
techniques applied [11-17].  95 
This integrated approach allows for comprehensive and accurate detection of deepfakes 96 
across modalities, outperforming traditional single-stream detectors. 97

                         1,1 Techniques for deepfake video detection 99 
 100 
The fabricated video faces purportedly undergo Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)  101 
and reenactment model manipulation advanced deepfake video detection techniques and 102 
rationale have evolved rapidly to keep pace with the sophistication of facial manipulation. 103 
Detection techniques have advanced beyond simple artifact examination to integrate 104 
sophisticated deep learning techniques that analyze the face manipulation from an 105 
extensive temporal and spatial perspective. 106 
Some of widely accepted video deepfake detection techniques are:  107 
•  CNN-Based Frame Analysis: CNNs such as XceptionNet and MesoNet analyze 108 
video frames capturing spatial artifacts like blending artifacts or unnatural 109 
textures. 110 
•  Hybrid CNN-LSTM Models: Frame features are isolated by CNNs, while LSTMs 111 
analyze sequential frames to capture motion discrepancies across time.  112 
•  3D CNNs and Spatiotemporal Networks: Video clips are processed as a 3D block 113 
enabling simultaneous spatial and temporal feature learning. 114 
•  Vision Transformers (ViTs): Global relations among patches of a frame are 115 
captured by transformer -based architectures which have outperformed other 116 
models in recent evaluations. 117 
•  Lip Sync and Audio -Visual Consistency Checks: These techniques identify 118 
articulation and timing disagreements by relating speech to the corresponding 119 
facial movements. 120 
•  Biological Signal Analysis: The model tries to find absent physiological signals, 121 
such as blinking or heartbeat, that may indicate life in the region of the face. 122 
•  Frequency Domain Analysis: Techniques like Fourier transforms are applied to 123 
expose forms of manipulation that are not visible in the spatial domain.  124 
•  Multi-Region and Ensemble Methods: These aggregate face region wise 125 
predictions or different types of models to enhance effectiveness. 126 
•  One-Class and Anomaly Detection: These models are trained only on real videos 127 
and detect any fake content as anomalous. 128

classification. 131 
     1.2 Techniques for deepfake audio detection 132 
The development of deepfake audio detection techniques has attracted a lot of attention 133 
because of the availability of new generative models like WaveNet, Tacotron, and voice 134 
conversion systems, which can create ultra -realistic synthetic speech. This has led 135 
researchers to examine various methods for detecting synthetic speech using traditional 136 
signal-based techniques as well as deep learning. 137 
 Various techniques used for deepfake audio detection are as follows. 138 
• MFCC Based Classifiers- (MFCC)- MFCCs, which are frequently used as features 139 
in SVMs or even deep neural networks, are known to capture pertinent features of 140 
speech. 141 
• Spectrogram and CNN: Speech synthesization 'fake' marks are also detected using 142 
CNNs on time-domain mel and log-spectrograms that are pre-obtained. This also 143 
includes detecting fakes on Mel spectrograms. 144 
• Raw Waveforms Modelling: Handcrafted feature extraction is not a necessity for 145 
end-to-end systems such as RawNet2 which processes audio through trained 146 
models. 147 
•  CRNN Models: These types of Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks 148 
(CRNN) which combines time and space consist of models that are able to handle 149 
persistence of detectably small alterations. 150 
•  RNNs, as well as BiLSTM models, are commonly appropriate for building 151 
sequence dependent models. 152 
• Frequency and Phase Analysis: Certain methods review the frequency spectrum 153 
or phase distortion to identify residue masquerade vocoders. 154 
•  Transformer Based Models: Models such as Wav2Vec 2.0 and AST (Audio 155 
Spectrogram Transformer) have used self-attention for spoofing detection. 156 
• GAN Fingerprint Detection: It detects artifacts of artificial synthesis from a specific 157 
generative model such as WaveGAN or MelGAN. 158 
• Self-supervised and Contrastive Learning: These methods improve feature 159 
learning through extensive available unlabelled data. 160

o Proposed System Architecture  176 
The proposed deepfake video detection system is based on a hybrid CNN -LSTM 177 
architecture designed to capture both spatial and temporal inconsistencies in manipulated 178 
facial content. The system comprises three main components: a CNN for feature extraction, 179 
a BiLSTM for temporal modelling, and a fully connected layer for classification. Fig.1 180 
depicts the overview of the proposed system. 181 
• Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) 182 
The CNN component is responsible for extracting spatial features from individual video 183 
frames. The architecture of the CNN is inspired by XceptionNet, a deep convolutional 184 
neural network known for its efficient feature extraction capabilities. The CNN consists of 185 
multiple convolutional layers, each followed by a batch normalization layer and a ReLU 186 
activation function. The convolutional layers learn to extract relevant features from the 187 
input frames, such as edges, textures, and facial features. The batch normalization layers 188 
help to stabilize the training process and improve the generalization performance of the 189 
network. The ReLU activation functions introduce non-linearity into the network, allowing 190 
it to learn complex patterns in the data. 191 
Adaptive Average Pooling is applied to reduce feature map dimensions . This helps to 192 
reduce the computational cost of the subsequent LSTM layer and also makes the system 193 
more robust to variations in the size and position of the face in the input frames.  194 
• Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) 195 
The BiLSTM component is responsible for modeling the temporal dependencies between 196 
the frame-level features extracted by the CNN. The BiLSTM network consists of two LSTM 197 
layers, one for processing the input sequence in the forward direction and the other for 198 

Sensors 2025, 25, x FOR PEER REVIEW 6 of 22  
 
processing it in the backward direction. This allows the network to capture both past and 199 
future temporal dependencies across the sequence of frames. The LSTM layers are 200 
designed to handle long-range dependencies in sequential data, making them well -suited 201 
for modelling the temporal dynamics of facial movements and expressions. The BiLSTM 202 
architecture enables the model to detect subtle inconsistencies in facial motion or lighting 203 
artifacts introduced during deepfake generation. 204 
• Fully Connected Layer 205 
The output of the BiLSTM is fed into a set of fully connected layers, followed by a sigmoid 206 
activation function at the output layer. The fully connected layers learn to map the 207 
temporal features extracted by the BiLSTM to a scalar probability score indicating the 208 
likelihood of the input being a deepfake. The sigmoid activation function ensures that the 209 
output score is between 0 and 1, representing the probability of the input being fake. A 210 
threshold (typically 0.5) is applied to this score for binary classification, determining 211 
whether the video is classified as REAL or FAKE [17-22]. 212

• Implementation Details 239 
The CNN-LSTM model was implemented using the PyTorch deep learning framework. 240 
The model was trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 and a batch 241 
size of 32. The training process was conducted on a GPU server using Google Colab.  242 
a. Model Initialization 243 
It starts with model architecture for CNN -LSTM initialization. The CNN Layers are built 244 
to extract spatial features applied to every input video frame recognizing important 245 
structures and patterns of the face. The generated  features are then passed to three 246 
Adaptive Average Pooling layers to shrink the dimensions of the feature maps while 247 
retaining prominent information. The generated output then goes into a Long Short Term 248 
Memory (LSTM) layer that serves in capturing temporal dependencies over a sequence is 249 
real or fake. After defining the architecture, the  model loads the pre -trained weights 250 
learned during training step and sets the model to evaluation mode to ensure that output 251 
remain same when we use this model for inference. 252 
b. Video Preprocessing 253 
Once the model has been initialized, the input video has to be loaded for processing. Since 254 
video is composed of 30 or more frames, uniform sampling is performed within a video 255 
at a rate of one frame per second to minimize computational overhead and to ensure 256 
frames contain useful segmentation. Then, it is pre -processed the extracted frames to fit 257 
the input format expected by the model. This can involve converting the frame to an RGB 258 

Sensors 2025, 25, x FOR PEER REVIEW 8 of 22  
 
format via the PIL library, resizing to a fixed resolution of 128x128 pixels and normalizing 259 
(by taking the mean and standard deviation of 0.5 for each channel to normalize the pixel 260 
intensity values between the range of  [-1,1]). These normalization values are used to 261 
render input stable and to boost model performance. The processed frame is  then 262 
transformed into a PyTorch tensor for use in the model. 263 
c. Frame-Level Inference 264 
After preprocessing all the frames, they are sent to the model one at a time for frame-level 265 
inference. First, each frame goes through the CNN layers to  capture spacial features that 266 
encode facial and contextual information. Adaptive Average Pooling is then used to 267 
convert these features into a fixed-size feature vector, independent of the input resolution. 268 
The pooled features are reshaped and fed into a LSTM (Long-Short Term Memory) layer, 269 
which examines how the features evolve temporally across the frames. Finally, the 270 
temporal output from LSTM is passed on to Fully Connected layers which are responsible 271 
for returning values i.e. raw prediction score (logit) for frames. The predicted output is a  272 
score indicating how confident is the model about whether the frame is authentic or fake.  273

The proposed system automatically detects deepfake audio by combining MFCC for 308 
spectral feature extraction and LSTM for temporal pattern recognition. This integration 309 
effectively captures both acoustic characteristics and sequential dependencies, enabling 310 
accurate classification of audio authenticity.          311 
This method is designed as a multi -stage pipeline that systematically processes raw audio 312 
to deliver an accurate classification. The system is developed with the goal of not only 313 
detecting whether an audio sample is real or fake, but also understanding how synthetic 314 
speech differs in temporal and spectral structure. It begins by enhancing the audio signal 315 
to remove unwanted noise and ensure consistent formatting, which is essential for reliable 316 

Sensors 2025, 25, x FOR PEER REVIEW 10 of 22  
 
analysis. The enhanced signal is then transformed into meaningful features using MFCC, 317 
which captures how humans perceive sound. These features are well -suited for deep 318 
learning models like LSTM, which are capable of detecting subtle, time-dependent patterns 319 
in speech. Once the LSTM processes the input, the model outputs a probability indicating 320 
the likelihood that the audio is fake. This score is compared to a threshold, and the result is 321 
classified accordingly. Overall, the pipeline offers a powerful, interpretable, and modular 322 
solution to audio deepfake-detection. 323 
To enhance the raw audio and make the signal more uniform and reliable for analysis by 324 
reducing noise and standardizing its temporal structure. A critical transformation that 325 
converts audio waveforms into compact, informative representations that highlight 326 
spectral patterns indicative of human speech characteristics. Through the use of an LSTM - 327 
based deep learning model that can capture temporal dependencies in speech sequences, 328 
enabling the detection of subtle anomalies typical in deepfake audio. Rather than producing 329 
a binary output, the model yields a probabilistic prediction, thereby allowing for flexible 330 
thresholding and enhancing interpretability. The system facilitates real -time, confidence- 331 
informed classification (REAL or FAKE), making it suitable for deployment in safety - 332 
critical-environments.[22-24] 333 
• Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients 334 
Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) are used to extract meaningful 335 
features from the audio signal by simulating the way humans perceive sound. After 336 
preprocessing the audio into short frames, each frame undergoes a Fourier 337 
Transform to reveal its frequency content, followed by the application of a Mel - 338 
scale filter bank that emphasizes perceptually important frequencies. The resulting 339 
energies are logarithmically compressed and transformed using the Discrete 340 
Cosine Transform (DCT) to produce compact, uncorrelated coefficients. These 341 
MFCCs form a two -dimensional feature matrix that captures both spectral and 342 
temporal properties of speech, making them ideal inputs for the LSTM model to 343 
distinguish between real and fake audio patterns. 344 
• Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) 345 
The LSTM network processes the sequence of MFCC feature vectors and captures 346 
temporal dependencies in the speech signal. A Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) is 347 
used to model the audio context from both past and future frames. This helps detect 348 
unnatural transitions, voice inconsistencies, and temporal anomalies characteristic 349 
of deepfake audio. The final hidden states from the LSTM are forwarded to dense 350 
layers for classification . 351

5. Conclusions 565 
This work introduces an effective and scalable deepfake detection framework that targets 566 
both audio and video modalities with state-of-the-art deep learning methods. Through the 567 
use of a CNN -LSTM model on face -aligned video frames obtained from datasets like 568 
FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF, and DFDC, the proposed method effectively identifies spatial 569 
and temporal inconsistencies with a detection accuracy of 94%. For speech -based forgery 570 
detection, Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) are fed into a BiLSTM network 571 
trained on the SceneFake dataset with a high classification accuracy of 98% on different 572 
signal-to-noise ratios and enhancement conditions. 573 
The two -modality architecture also exhibits excellent generalization properties among 574 
manipulation approaches and various input situations. Having the two modules 575 
consolidated into an end -user accessible web -based interface even further supports the 576 
practical feasibility of the system to be used in real -world contexts, facilitating immediate 577 
and accurate deepfake detection through end users. 578 
This research makes a valuable contribution to the area of multimedia forensics by closing 579 
the gap between research and deployment, with a robust framework for 580 
futureextensions.Future improvements can include the use of attention mechanisms, 581 
Sensors 2025, 25, x FOR PEER REVIEW 19 of 22  
 
ensemble architectures, and sophisticated data augmentation techniques to enhance 582 
robustness and flexibility. In general, this research highlights the importance of integrating 583 
spectral and temporal modeling for thorough deepfake detection and opens the door to 584 
scalable, real-world applications in synthetic media analysis. 585 
6. Future Scope 586 
Future work on the DeepShield system will extend to consistently detecting threats in live 587 
video streams and working toward robustness across varying video qualities and 588 
conditions. It will also focus on optimized latency and scheduling for  mobile and low - 589 
latency deployments. Model accuracy can be improved by utilizing sophisticated 590 
methodologies like CNN with LSTM for video and MFCC  with LSTM for audio using 591 
transfer learning. Other useful techniques include attention mechanisms (Transformers), 592 
GANs for  synthetic data generation, multimodal fusion (e.g., audio and video), and, 593 
sometimes, better anomaly detection using autoencoders that you could check for RNN 594 
and TCN integration too We further investigate the effectiveness of different structures in 595 
video (3D CNNs) and audio (CRNNs) domains. This could involve exploring techniques 596 
such as meta -learning, domain adaptation, and adversarial training. We will further 597 
investigate ensemble learning and feature fusion across different pretrained models  to 598 
improve robustness. Finally, a combination of ensuring ethical usage and  collaboration 599 
with social media platforms and regulatory bodies will be essential as the world combats 600 
deepfake misinformation. Efforts could be made to reduce the computational cost of the 601 
system. This could involve exploring techniques such as model compression, 602 
quantization, and pruning. 603
